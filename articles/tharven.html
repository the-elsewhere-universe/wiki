The Tharven is a massive Orran herbivore, distinguished by the broad protective architecture of its neck and shoulder shield.

Form

The shield is not a flat plate but a strongly modelled anatomical structure, widening from the neck and integrating with the animal’s shoulders. Bulls and cows differ in proportion and display, while retaining the same unmistakable silhouette.

The animal’s name preserves this most immediate impression. Tarw-en is understood as “broad-necked” or “spreading-neck”; the English form, Tharven, follows its established Orran pronunciation.

Social life

Tharven gather in groups known in English as wards. The term catches both their social organisation and the visual effect of adults sheltering calves within a moving perimeter. Adult males, females and young are referred to as bulls, cows and calves; Orran equivalents have not yet been established.

At rest, a ward can look less like a herd than a low wall assembled across the land.

In culture

Across Orra the Tharven carries associations of protection and mourning. Its cultural meaning grows from behaviour rather than abstraction: bodies placed between danger and the vulnerable, and the close presence of the ward around loss.

Editorial note

Precise habitat, diet and reproductive ecology remain open. This entry records established anatomy, terminology and cultural associations only.
