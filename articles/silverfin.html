Small, reflective and spectacular only in number, Silverfins are the biological engine behind many of Orra’s greatest migrations.

An ordinary foundation

Silverfins are fish-like schooling organisms around fifteen to twenty-five centimetres long. Their highly reflective scales turn vast shoals into silver flashes visible from the cliffs above.

They support Deepwing migrations, Tidehawk hunts, Greyback feeding, Glasswing breeding and the coastal fishing economy. Orra’s most monumental natural events depend on this relatively ordinary animal.

When the ocean stirs

Cold, nutrient-rich water rises along the submerged continuation of Orra’s escarpment. Spring phytoplankton blooms bring immense Silverfin shoals close to the coast. Through summer the system reaches maximum abundance; in autumn the Deepwing migration peaks before winter empties the water again.

Orra appears almost to breathe through the year, and the Silverfin shoals are the first visible pulse.

Staple of the coast

Silverfin has historically been an everyday food rather than a luxury: eaten fresh, grilled, smoked, salted, pickled, fermented and preserved against winter. The animal connects household life and maritime culture to the larger ecology offshore.

A working name

The organism’s ecological role and basic form are canonical. “Silverfin” remains a working name, and its finer biology has not yet been fixed. This entry will change as that record develops.
